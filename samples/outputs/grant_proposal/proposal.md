# BRIDGE: Community Digital Learning

**Fictional draft · KA220 cooperation partnership · human review required.**

## Summary

BRIDGE: Community Digital Learning proposes a practical cooperation programme for adult educators. Adult educators need reusable, accessible methods for teaching digital skills. Partners will co-design a reusable toolkit, test it with local educator groups and document the adaptations needed in different settings. The project combines a shared needs-validation protocol, accessible learning resources, supported local pilots and a transparent evaluation process. Its draft outputs will be reviewed by practitioners before wider dissemination. This fictional application exercise uses an illustrative budget and partner roles. It does not assert that any applicant is eligible or that a funding call has been checked. The consortium must substantiate the need and approve the final application.

## Needs

The starting challenge is: Adult educators need reusable, accessible methods for teaching digital skills. This is a supplied problem statement, not completed needs research. Before finalising the intervention, each partner will consult educators and prospective participants, record accessibility barriers and compare existing provision. A common interview guide and consent procedure will support comparison while leaving room for local context. Partners will distinguish observed needs from assumptions, summarise the evidence in a needs matrix and use it to prioritise modules. Recruitment targets and learning outcomes will be revised when this evidence is available. The application should cite those findings rather than invent baseline statistics or endorsements.

## Objectives

- Validate educator needs and agree observable learning outcomes.
- Co-design and pilot six accessible learning modules with practitioner feedback.
- Publish an evaluated adaptation handbook and a partner-owned continuation plan.

## Partnership

The supplied partnership is P1 (EL): coordination and adult learning; P2 (IT): accessible learning design; P3 (ES): piloting and evaluation. A steering group will maintain a decision log, review progress against deliverables and resolve changes to scope or resources. Each work-package lead will coordinate a small delivery group with a named deputy. Partners will agree responsibilities, accessibility checks, evidence retention and review dates before activities start. Participation in this fictional plan is not a statement of legal commitment; actual institutions must confirm capacity, eligibility and their authorised representatives before submission.

## Methodology

Delivery follows a co-design, pilot, review and transfer cycle. Educators will help turn the validated needs into short learning tasks with accessible alternatives. Partners will review draft modules against a shared checklist, then facilitate small local pilots and collect consented feedback. Review meetings will compare implementation notes across sites, decide which changes are supported by evidence and version the toolkit. Materials will include facilitator notes, adaptable activities and assessment examples. A final transfer workshop will test whether another educator can use the handbook without the original authors present. Evaluation findings will shape the final release.

## Evaluation

The evaluation plan will link each objective to a defined output and an observable outcome. A baseline and follow-up educator self-assessment will be supplemented by task demonstrations and facilitator observations. The team will predefine indicators, collection windows and responsibility for missing data. Quantitative summaries will be reported alongside participant feedback and accessibility findings. Reviewers will record limitations and avoid attributing every observed change to the project. A separate quality review will check deliverable completeness, evidence provenance and the usability of the adaptation handbook. Indicator targets require agreement after needs validation.

## Impact and sustainability

Expected benefits are more consistent educator practice, reusable accessible materials and stronger cooperation between the participating organisations. These are intended outcomes rather than measured achievements. The dissemination plan will identify specific practitioner audiences, appropriate channels and opportunities to demonstrate the materials in use. Each partner will nominate an owner for maintaining its adapted resources and explain the staff time needed after the funded period. The final handbook will distinguish mandatory project elements from local adaptations and include evidence limitations. Long-term uptake will require later follow-up rather than an assumed multiplier.

## Risks and mitigations

- Recruitment delays: agree reserve recruitment channels and review participation early.
- Accessibility gaps: test draft resources with users and budget time for revision.
- Partner delivery delays: use deputies, milestone reviews and a documented change process.
